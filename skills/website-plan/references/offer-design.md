# Offer design grounded in proof

Read when the page presents packages, bonuses, guarantees, promotions or changes to the offer. These are tools for explaining real value. Proof over promise governs all of them.

## Start with the real exchange

List what the customer gets, what they pay or provide, what is excluded and what happens next. Connect the components to the customer's desired result, confidence, time and effort. An attractive offer makes that exchange easy to judge; it doesn't need exaggerated savings or a long list of ordinary tasks renamed as bonuses.

Describe the result from the customer's point of view with concrete, supported details. Use their situation before the service, the current decision and life with the result where those help understanding. Keep the scale appropriate to the purchase. Don't manufacture a nightmare, tell everyone past failures were someone else's fault, or promise social approval.

## Packages and bonuses

Make the confirmed contents visible, grouping related items so a scanner can see the scope. Consider whether a real tool, checklist, service or resource removes an objection, reduces work or solves the next likely problem. A bonus needs a use, not just a name.

For a relevant bonus, establish:

- what it is and whether it is included, optional or conditional;
- which specific customer problem it solves and how it supports the outcome;
- why it is useful, with a source, example or documented experience;
- its origin or development story only if known and relevant;
- any eligibility, availability or delivery conditions.

Use a clear name that conveys the function or benefit. Tools and checklists may reduce effort better than extra exercises, but choose for the actual task. Don't invent a cash value, a former selling price or a claim that the bonuses are worth more than the main service. A guarantee can reduce perceived risk only when its real coverage, exclusions and claim process are clear.

## Urgency and scarcity

Use a deadline, limited capacity or exclusive access only when the business confirms the condition and can honour it. Keep a source for the start/end dates, actual limit, who qualifies and what changes after the offer ends. Where a time matters, make the time zone clear. Plan what the page should show after expiry.

A seasonal name can explain the occasion for an offer. Renaming the same continuously available deal every month doesn't create an actual deadline. Don't reset countdowns, invent remaining places or imply that a normal inclusion disappears tonight. Don't publish expired incentives.

## Proposals outside the brief

If changing the price, bundle, bonus or guarantee would improve the offer, put a concrete suggestion in "Gaps and decisions": the proposed change, which problem it solves, and what the owner needs to confirm about delivery and terms. Ask focused questions when needed. Until confirmed, keep the existing offer in the site copy and leave the proposed terms out of claims, CTAs and timers.

## Final check

Can a customer tell what is included, why it helps, what they must do, what it costs and what happens next? Can the owner substantiate every result, value, guarantee and limit? If a tactic makes the page feel like constant pressure, cut it and let the evidence carry the decision.
