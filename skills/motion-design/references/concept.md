# Concept: the idea first, then the motion

Read before the storyboard. A model's first idea is the average of thousands of videos: the logo arrives with a glow, a phone spins in the air, every line of text slides up from the bottom. Correct, and forgotten a second later. This file gives you a method for an idea nobody else would have had, and then a list of moves that make an impression.

## 1. The essence as a verb

Describe what the product does for the customer with one physical verb, not an adjective. "Modern" gives you no picture; "splits" gives you a line that branches straight away. The verb gives you the language of transitions, the direction of motion and the pace of the whole video.

| Product | Verb | What it means on screen |
|---|---|---|
| ReviewLink (a link for reviews) | leads, splits | a dot that leads the viewer through the whole video; one line that branches into two paths |
| a laundry with pick-up and delivery | folds | each scene folds into the next like a sheet; the logo is folded from one piece of fabric |
| an appointment booking app | arranges | the scattered blocks of a day arrange themselves into a calendar |
| an accounting office | puts in order | a chaos of sheets, numbers and arrows turns into one even line |
| a paint shop | floods | colour spreads across the frame and becomes the background of the next scene |
| a renovation company | renews | an old, ragged line redraws itself cleanly, layer by layer |

If the verb fits every company in the industry ("helps", "supports"), keep looking.

## 2. Reject the obvious

Before you propose a direction, write down the three ideas that came first. They are the average, so reject them, but give each one a replacement straight away: a ban alone just pushes the model to the next template instead of producing something of its own.

| Obvious idea | Replacement |
|---|---|
| the logo appears in the centre with a glow | the logo assembles from its own shapes, or emerges from a motion that ran through the whole video |
| a phone spins in the air next to a list of features | one concrete use of the product, step by step, with a touch point and its result |
| every line of text slides up from the bottom the same way | typography as architecture: words are windows, a floor or the camera's path |
| particles, flares, glitch | motion that comes from the product's verb |
| a dissolve between scenes | a cut on a shape, colour, direction or an object that carries over |
| the usual look: Inter, a purple gradient, a neon glow, cream with a serif and terracotta | the brand's colours and font, or a palette and typeface decided with `references/look.md` |
| counters like "10,000+ customers" | real numbers or none; numbers as choreography, not decoration |

## 3. Three clearly different directions

Propose three directions that differ in world, not in colour. Each in five lines:

```md
**Direction: <name>**
Idea: <one sentence, with the product's verb>
World: <material, camera, space>
Look: <palette as named values, typeface and why; references/look.md>
Signature: <one move only this brand has>
Transitions: <the language of the cuts>
```

Worlds to choose and mix:

- **Typographic:** letters are architecture. The camera travels across words, and letters become windows onto the next scenes.
- **One line:** the whole video is one continuous stroke that draws the interface, branches and curls into the logo.
- **The mark as a world:** the logo's shapes build a 3D space the camera flies through, and transitions have the shape of the mark.
- **Macro and micro:** jumps in scale from a city map to a single star on a screen and back; every scene sits inside an object of the previous one.
- **The product taken apart:** the interface splits into layers in 3D, shows what it's made of and reassembles.
- **Illustrated:** characters from the brand's illustrations interact with the interface: they sit on a bar of a chart, push a card, catch a falling star.
- **Editorial:** frames like magazine spreads, huge typography, a render or illustration as the background, cuts on the beat.
- **Data as choreography:** numbers drive the motion; bars grow and become stairs the camera climbs.

Pick the boldest direction that is true to the product and can be built in HTML in reasonable time. The swap test: would another model make almost the same thing from a similar brief? If so, the direction is too safe.

## 4. Moves that make an impression

Three or four big moves per 30 s are enough; the rest is clean craft. Too many tricks is noise. Implementations: `references/techniques.md`, sections in brackets.

| Move | When | How (techniques section) |
|---|---|---|
| A transition shaped like the mark | when the logo has distinctive shapes | the mark's shapes grow from different places and cover the frame (3) |
| Letters as windows | the line is short and strong | the next scene shows through huge letters, which grow until they fill the frame (4) |
| Infinite zoom | the product has nesting (a link on a phone, a phone in a hand, a hand in a city) | every scene sits in a small object of the previous one (2) |
| Shape morph | one symbol should turn into another | a dot into a star, a star into the shape of the mark (7) |
| One line | the product connects, leads or flows | an SVG path draws the interface and turns into the next scene (7) |
| Taking it apart in layers | you show how something works inside | an interface card separates in 3D into background, text and buttons (6) |
| An interface from blocks | the product screen should come together in front of the viewer | rectangles fly in with a spring and assemble into the screen (5) |
| Split screen | two paths, or before and after | two halves of the frame play at once and join at the end (3) |
| Freeze frame with a note | a detail you can't see in motion | the action stops, a line labels the detail, the action resumes (7) |
| Kinetic grid | many elements at once (industries, features) | tiles turn in a wave and reveal a word or an image (5) |
| The world changes colour | the climax or a change of chapter | the whole frame takes on the accent colour, once or twice per video (3) |
| Camera over a floor | the interface should "lie down" | the screen tilted 55–65° on the X axis, the camera flies over it (6) |
| A perfect loop | README, social reels | the last frame is the first one (9) |
| Broken rhythm | after a run of fast cuts | three cuts on the beats, then a second of stillness: silence in the picture is an accent too (9) |

## 5. Dramaturgy

A video has a curve of energy, not a list of scenes:

1. **Hook (0–2 s):** the strongest image or a question. In reels the first frame already has content, because it's the thumbnail and plays without sound.
2. **Build-up:** the product in action, on one example.
3. **A surprise every 5–8 s:** a change of scale, colour, perspective or direction. A viewer who knows what comes next stops watching.
4. **Climax (about 70–80% of the video):** the biggest move, often the world changing colour or the signature.
5. **Rest:** a calm end card that stays for 2–3 s and closes the motif from the opening.

Rough lengths: logo intro 4–8 s, reel 10–20 s, showreel and promo 35–50 s, explainer up to 90 s. In a reel under 15 s the curve shrinks to a hook, one build-up, the climax and the end card; one surprise is enough.

## 6. An honest review of the ReviewLink showreel

What worked:

- The verb "leads" gave the motif: the dot over the "i" opens the video, turns into an orange world, shrinks into a touch point, taps a star and comes back on the end card.
- A signature from a brand element: three 3D cubes assemble into the Design House mark.
- The product shown on one tap: the customer taps the fifth star, and a line branches into two paths.

What was weakest, that is the bar for next time:

- The benefits montage was five similar cards, "text on the left, interface on the right", joined by different transitions. The transitions were spectacular, but the world stood still. Better: the line from the opening keeps going and draws the QR code, the report and the carousel one after another, or everything happens inside one interface taken apart in layers, with the camera travelling through it.
- The manifesto on orange was typographically clean but predictable. Letters as windows onto the next scenes would have turned it into a transition.
